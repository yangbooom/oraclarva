# L1 motor-neuron to muscle crosswalk v1

## Result

The checked-in map joins two parts of the same primary publication:

1. Zarin et al. Supplementary file 1 supplies 61 CATMAID skeleton IDs.
2. The public L1EM CATMAID API currently resolves 58 of those IDs to names.
3. Zarin et al. Table 1 maps the named motor-neuron classes to spatial muscle
   group, target muscle number and synonym, nerve, and synapse type.

The deterministic join produces 58 anatomical projections: 56 in A1 and the
left/right MN25 pair in A2. The source states that MN25 is absent in A1 and was
therefore reconstructed in A2. All resolved projections retain left/right side
and their original CATMAID skeleton ID.

Three supplementary skeleton IDs do not resolve to names in the current public
endpoint: `4461322`, `14086623`, and `14197913`. Their supplement color and their
position in the file are not sufficient evidence for assigning one of the three
Type-II VUM classes. They remain explicitly unresolved.

## Evidence files

- `zarin_2019_catmaid_snapshot.json` stores all 61 IDs, the 58 API-resolved
  names, the three unresolved IDs, source checksums, retrieval date, and CATMAID
  namespace.
- `zarin_2019_table1.json` is a machine-readable transcription of Table 1.
- `l1_motor_map_v1.json` is generated by `tools/compile_l1_motor_map.py`.

Run `python tools/compile_l1_motor_map.py --check` to detect stale generated
data, and `oraclarva-motor-map-audit` to inspect coverage and blockers.

## Research-mode closed-loop integration

The embodied v0 research fixture now instantiates one LIF identity neuron for
each of the 58 resolved CATMAID skeletons. The 56 A1 identities receive the
A1 aggregate motor-pool event and their normalized spike fraction drives the
already calibrated A1 aggregate muscle actuator. The two A2 MN25 identities
are recorded for diagnostics only because they are not complete A2 coverage.

This does not fill the null gains in `l1_motor_map_v1.json`. The common
identity input and normalized A1 pooling are explicitly `MODEL_FITTED`, the
three unresolved skeletons remain absent, and no identity-specific attachment
or force is executed. The runtime therefore retains `release_validated: false`.

An A1 identity-population lesion is a causal audit: the aggregate A1 pool
still spikes, but A1 contraction/proprioception and downstream T3 recruitment
disappear. This establishes that the research body passes through the named
identity layer without claiming a release-ready neuromuscular map.

## Why this map still cannot move the release body

Table 1 establishes anatomical targets, not calibrated force gains, activation
dynamics, or 3D attachment geometry. Assigning every listed target a gain of 1
would be a modeling assumption disguised as a measurement. Likewise, mapping
the spatial groups directly onto the existing longitudinal/transverse test
channels would erase oblique and broad innervation.

For that reason every biological projection has `weight: null` and
`muscle_group: null`. The release gate requires both fields to be backed by an
observed or fitted source before projection into body physics. Synthetic values
remain available only to explicit software fixtures.

## Next scientific milestone

Create a per-muscle A1 geometry model from published or newly measured origin,
insertion, and orientation data; then fit activation-to-force dynamics against
muscle shortening or force recordings. Only after that should identity-specific A1 neural outputs drive individual
A1
fiber deformation; the current research proxy drives only the aggregate axial
actuator. Homologous copies across A2-A7 must be labeled derived or hypothetical
unless segment-specific IDs and targets are independently audited.
